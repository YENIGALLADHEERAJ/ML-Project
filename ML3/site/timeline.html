1950s / SHOULDER

Replacing the humeral head

Early shoulder hemiarthroplasty

Charles Neer’s 1955 paper described articular replacement of the humeral head. The implant replaced the ball on the upper arm side of the joint, rather than both sides of the shoulder.

These are original, simplified vector reconstructions of design concepts. They are not scans, dimensionally accurate CAD replicas, specific commercial-device identifications or a surgical guide. Dates refer to the cited milestones, not the first appearance of every design feature.

From artifact to algorithm

A design can change.
The joint stays the same.

A conventional shoulder stem and a reverse shoulder assembly can look very different, yet both belong to the shoulder. Implant identity is more specific than anatomical region.

This timeline explains the visual context. Our live checkpoints still output only Hip, Knee or Shoulder. They cannot date an implant, recognize these historical designs or select a device for a patient.

Why source control is helpful

If a dataset contains only one joint and one family of appearances, a model may learn those source-associated cues. Our benchmark tests what happens when the source changes. The timeline is educational context, not proof of a causal implant effect.
