Limitations

What this study cannot show

Stated plainly, because the headline result is easy to over-read.

In plain words

What this study cannot tell you, such as whether the AI works in hospitals. Read this before you quote any number.

New here? Start with the 2-minute guide →

One decoupled source

The decoupled test rests on FracAtlas (three hospitals; 55 test images). It cannot separate general source effects from FracAtlas-specific ones.

Provisional knee labels

94 FracAtlas knee images were labelled by an AI model and are not clinician-verified. A hip/shoulder-only sensitivity analysis shows the gap narrows to 0.10–0.34 without the knee class.

Implants are a confound, not a cause

Hip and shoulder sources are implant images; no knee source is. We cannot attribute failure to implants. The inpainting proxy is weak.

Public-data provenance

Some images were compiled from publications or web pages; patient-level leakage within a source cannot be excluded; licences are heterogeneous.

Limited compute

Three epochs at 160 px on a 2 GB GPU; ConvNeXt-T has one seed. The study did not test whether longer training or higher-resolution inputs change the gap.

No clinical validation

The study concerns evaluation of joint recognition on public data — not deployment, fracture detection or implant identification.

Targeted literature search

Not systematic; the absence of a directly comparable study is a statement about our search only.

Self-recorded freeze

Pre-registration was hash-recorded locally, not deposited in an independent registry.

The full list (14 items) is in the manuscript. Demo outputs here inherit all of these limits.

Project scopeThe models classify Hip, Knee or Shoulder. This project evaluates how joint-recognition models are tested; disease prediction and implant-brand identification are outside the implemented task.
