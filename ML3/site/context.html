A short introduction

What is medical implantation?

Implantation is the placement of a device or tissue in or on the body. Orthopaedic implants can replace joint surfaces or help support damaged bone.

A hip replacement typically includes a socket component, a ball and a stem. Knee and shoulder replacements have different shapes and components. These differences make radiographs useful for studying anatomy and implant appearance.

Educational context: FDA: implants and prosthetics ↗ · AAOS: joint replacement ↗

Our model names the joint.

It does not identify implant brands, recommend an implant, diagnose a condition or assess implant safety.

Why implants appear in the story

Source can become
a hidden answer key.

The Kaggle hip source and UCI shoulder source contain implant radiographs. Other sources differ in age, framing, equipment and image properties.

When one source mostly corresponds to one joint, a network can exploit source-associated cues. Holding that source out tests a harder question. Implant presence is a plausible confound; this study does not show it causes the failures.
